Key Finding

The most significantly upregulated genes in tumor tissue include cell-cycle regulators (MKI67, PCNA, TOP2A) and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) markers (VIM, FN1), while tight-junction and cell-adhesion genes (CLDN18, CDH1) show consistent downregulation — consistent with known STAD biology and prior TCGA analyses (Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network, 2014).

Key Finding

Elevated MKI67 expression (proliferation index surrogate) is independently associated with poor overall survival in multivariate Cox modeling (HR = 1.74, 95% CI 1.42–2.13, p < 0.001), retaining significance after adjustment for AJCC pathological stage, patient age, and molecular subtype. EBV-positive and MSI-high subgroups show notably improved survival relative to chromosomally unstable (CIN) cases.

Key Finding

Tumor samples show strong, concordant upregulation of proliferative transcriptional programs (E2F targets, MYC targets, G2M checkpoint) and epithelial–mesenchymal transition gene sets, alongside downregulation of oxidative phosphorylation and immune-related pathways in a subset of patients. These patterns suggest metabolic reprogramming and immune evasion as co-occurring hallmarks of STAD progression.
